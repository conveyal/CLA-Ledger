# Privacy — CSA Contributor License Agreement signatures

CSA's overall privacy practices, your data-protection rights, and how to contact
us are set out in the **CSA Privacy Notice**:
<https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/legal/privacy-notice/>
(contact: dpo@cloudsecurityalliance.org). That notice governs.

This page adds only the detail **specific to signing a Contributor License
Agreement (CLA)** — what the signing process records, that it is public, and how
long it is kept — because those facts are particular to this repository (the
"CLA-Ledger") and are not covered by the general notice.

## What signing records, and that it is public

When you sign a CLA in GitHub, your signature file in this **public** repository
records:

- your **GitHub username** at the time of signing;
- your **GitHub numeric account ID** — the identity of record, because usernames
  can be renamed but the numeric ID is permanent; it is GitHub-assigned metadata,
  visible via GitHub's API;
- the **CLA version** you signed and the **date**.

Because the file is added by a pull request, the public repository history
records the commit that added or updated it, and GitHub records the associated
pull request metadata. That linkage is repository and platform metadata — not a
field you enter in the file.

The **signature file** does not record your legal name, email address, or
employer — only the identifiers above. (Your git commit metadata is separate; see
below.) Your username and numeric ID are already public the moment you contribute
to a public repository.

Because this is a public repository, your signature record is public and may be
cloned, mirrored, indexed, or cached by others. We publish it deliberately, as a
transparent record of who has agreed to the CLA.

Separately, git stamps every commit with the author name and email your local git
is configured with — including the commit that adds your signature file. CSA does
not ask you to provide a legal name or email in the signature file, does not
verify commit author name or email, and does not rely on them for CLA identity.
The identity that matters for signing is the authenticated GitHub account that
opens the pull request: its login and numeric account ID. Commit author fields
are self-asserted metadata controlled by the contributor. Because this repository
is public, whatever name and email you put there become part of the permanent
public history. If you would rather not publish a real name or email, configure
git with a chosen name and a private or GitHub-provided
`…@users.noreply.github.com` address before committing.

## Why, and how long we keep it

The record evidences your agreement to the CLA and the resulting license grant.
Because that grant is **perpetual and irrevocable**, the record is kept
**indefinitely** as evidence of it. Deleting it would not end the rights you
granted — only remove the evidence that you granted them — so signature records
are not removed on request. Where a request can be honored without losing that
evidentiary value, we will consider it.

## Providing this information

Signing — and so providing these GitHub identifiers — is a **precondition** to
having your contributions accepted under the CLA. You are not obliged to; the
only consequence is that CSA cannot accept your contributions. You remain free
to take part in discussion and issues without signing.

## Your rights, requests, and questions

For your data-protection rights, any request, or a complaint, see the **CSA
Privacy Notice** at <https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/legal/privacy-notice/> or
contact **dpo@cloudsecurityalliance.org**.
